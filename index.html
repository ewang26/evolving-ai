01 — The Premise

AI as a major evolutionary transition

AI can be seen as a major evolutionary transition, of the same kind as the origin of cells, multicellular organisms, animal societies, and human language and culture. In each of those cases, a new system of inheritance or a new level of individuality emerged.

Evolutionary science has learned a great deal from these transitions, but little of it is being applied to AI. One reason for this gap is that for most of the twentieth century, evolutionary science was confined to the study of genetic evolution. The study of other evolutionary processes, such as cultural evolution and evolutionary algorithms, is much more recent and only slightly predates the AI revolution.

Modern evolutionary science is specific about the conditions under which groups of self-interested parts, from cells to human societies, come to function as cooperative wholes. Those conditions are directly relevant to systems of AI agents, especially superintelligent ones, and the societies that include them. We expect the gathering to result in collaborations and projects with lasting influence on how this transition unfolds.

02 — The Topics

Our fundamental focus areas

  1. i

    AI as a Major Evolutionary Transition

    What can previous major evolutionary transitions teach us about AI, and what evidence would show that a genuinely new system of inheritance or individuality is emerging?

  2. ii

    From Adaptive Agents to Adaptive Wholes

    What evolutionary mechanisms can transform independently optimizing AI agents into coherent, cooperative systems, and keep their collective goals compatible with human flourishing?

  3. iii

    Regulation, Cheating, and Systemic Breakdown

    How do adaptive systems detect and suppress components that benefit at the expense of the whole, and what can cancer, immunity, development, and conflict suppression teach us about agentic AI?

  4. iv

    Human–AI Coevolution

    How will humans and AI systems reshape one another's cognition, culture, values, capabilities, and environments through repeated feedback and cultural transmission?

  5. v

    Steering Open-Ended AI Ecosystems

    What practical strategies can achieve goals of diversity, innovation, and evolvability while shaping the selection pressures acting on agents, companies, markets, and states toward socially beneficial outcomes?

03 — The Gathering

A select group of the renowned experts from two communities

AI Research

Members of the AI community who see the need for an evolutionary perspective on the technology they are building and lines of research they are directing.

Evolutionary science

Members of the evolutionary science community who have already begun thinking about and working on AI.

Domain-specific topics like security and alignment from AI or natural selection from evolutionary science are important but are already discussed elsewhere in the two communities. This gathering is designed to take full advantage of the combination of the two fields, with breakout groups on each topic guided by leaders in that area.

04 — Fellowships

The first Evolving AI Fellowship cohort

We are providing fellowships for the next generation of students, early-career researchers, and builders to join the gathering as the first Evolving AI Fellowship cohort. The fellowship is fully funded, including travel, accommodation, and meals. You can apply as an individual or as a small team of two or three with a history of working together on research, building, or both.

An intimate gathering

Invited attendees include MacArthur Fellows, technology entrepreneurs, authors of best-selling books, and professors from around the world. The gathering is intimate.

Long-term projects with peers and experts

Fellows work on long-term projects with peers and invited experts. The goal is work that goes beyond an ordinary research paper.

Teams each submit a separate application, with a brief note on why you are applying together, your history of working together, and what you hope to accomplish as a group.
