Evolving AI

A Gathering to Catalyze Modern Evolutionary Thinking in the AI Community

Two communities — artificial intelligence and evolutionary science — convened at Harvard to accelerate a reaction already underway.

Artificial intelligence is a turning point in human history — a major evolutionary transition.

Artificial intelligence can be described as a major evolutionary transition, or MET — comparable to earlier transitions in human evolution, such as the emergence of spoken language, and in biological evolution, such as the rise of multicellular organisms and ultrasocial animal societies. Yet modern evolutionary science, including what we have learned from these other transitions, is not yet being leveraged in the field of AI.

There is a reason for this vacuum of knowledge. Evolutionary science was confined to the study of genetic evolution for most of the twentieth century. The study of other evolutionary processes — human cultural evolution, and evolutionary algorithms on computers — is far more recent, and only slightly predates the AI revolution itself.

There is an urgent need to catalyze the integration of these two revolutions. Modern evolutionary science speaks clearly about the special conditions required for societies of all sorts to function as cooperative units — including societies infused by AI. This gathering exists to bring those conditions to light.

MET — Major Evolutionary Transition The framework that connects the emergence of language, multicellularity, and ultrasociality — and now situates artificial intelligence as the newest such transition in the history of life on earth.

Five threads, one evolving conversation.

The questions we will hold in the room over three days — where evolutionary theory meets the practice of building intelligent systems.

01Human–AI cooperationHow can humans and intelligent systems coordinate toward socially beneficial outcomes?
02Evolution of agentic systemsWhat dynamics (e.g., cooperation, competition, and organization) emerge among interacting AI agents?
03Open-ended learningHow can we develop AI that continuously generates novel and useful capabilities without fixed objectives?
04Biological and machine learningWhich capabilities of biological learning are clearly missing from current AI, and how can humans use AI to expand their own capabilities?
05Human adaptation to superintelligenceHow can human expertise, judgment, and agency remain valuable as AI capabilities advance?

Tinbergen's four questions.

Four complementary bodies of knowledge, needed for a full understanding of every product of evolution — from a birdsong to a language model.

Function

Why?

The adaptive purpose a trait serves — what it is for.

History

Whence?

The evolutionary lineage — how it came to be, over deep time.

Mechanism

How?

The immediate machinery that makes it work in the present.

Development

From what?

How it assembles and unfolds across a single lifetime.
